PG Management Software: 10 Features to Check Before You Buy

"PG management software" is a crowded search term, and a lot of what shows up is generic property software with a PG label stuck on it. Here's what to actually check before you commit.

B Basera Team 11 Aug 2026 8 min read

A PG isn't a flat, and it isn't a hotel. It bills per bed, not per unit; residents share rooms; deposits are small and personal, not bank-verified security amounts; and most owners are running the whole thing from a phone, not a back office. Software built for residential property management or hotel PMS often gets these basics wrong — here's the checklist that actually matters for a PG or hostel.

1. Bed-level, not just room-level, tracking

You need to see occupancy down to the individual bed — which one's vacant, which one's reserved for someone moving in next week, which one's mid-transfer. Room-level tracking alone can't tell you if a triple-sharing room has one empty bed or three, and that's the number you actually rent against.

2. Per-bed billing that handles sharing

Rent in a PG is priced by the bed, and two residents sharing a room may be on different plans (different move-in dates, different rent amounts, different mess opt-ins). If the software can only generate one invoice per room, you'll be doing the math by hand anyway.

3. Proration that's actually automatic

A resident who moves in on the 18th shouldn't be billed for a full month, and a room transfer mid-month should split cleanly between the old and new room. This is one of the most common places spreadsheet-run PGs quietly lose money — either overbilling and creating a dispute, or underbilling and never noticing.

4. A resident-facing app, not just an owner dashboard

If residents can't see their own rent due, complaints status or mess menu, every question routes back to you — over a call or a WhatsApp message. A resident app that shows rent due and lets them pay and raise complaints removes you as the middleman on a dozen small interactions a day.

5. KYC and document storage that's actually secure

ID proof, photos and address documents are sensitive — they shouldn't sit in a WhatsApp media folder or a shared Google Drive with no access control. Check whether documents are stored against the specific resident and bed, and whether they're encrypted and access-controlled, not just "uploaded somewhere." See our guide to PG resident KYC for what's worth collecting in the first place.

6. Deposit tracking with a real ledger

A security deposit isn't a single number — it can be collected in installments, partially refunded mid-tenancy, and finally settled against deductions at move-out. Software that only stores "deposit amount: ₹X" as a static field can't handle any of that without you tracking the adjustments separately.

7. Complaints with a visible status, not a message thread

"I told the manager about the leaking tap" is not the same as a complaint with a status residents can check. A simple open → in-progress → resolved flow, visible to the resident who raised it, cuts down on the "any update?" follow-ups significantly.

8. Multi-property support from day one

Even if you run one PG today, check whether the software can handle a second property without a re-setup. If you're already managing more than one, this isn't optional — see managing multiple PG properties for what breaks without it.

9. Tenant data isolation you can actually trust

Your residents' phone numbers, ID documents and payment history are the most sensitive data in your business. Ask directly: is one PG's data isolated from another's at the database level, or just hidden in the UI? The honest answer matters more than the marketing page.

10. Pricing that scales with occupancy, not against it

A flat monthly fee regardless of how full you are punishes you exactly when you need the software most — during a slow season with more vacant beds. Pricing per occupied bed means your bill drops automatically when a bed sits empty, instead of staying fixed while your revenue falls.

Worth checking before a demo Ask any vendor to show you, live: adding a bed, prorating a mid-month move-in, and approving a UPI payment screenshot. If any of those three take more than a minute, the software wasn't built around how a PG actually runs day to day.
The right question isn't "does it have a feature list" — it's "does it match how rent, beds and residents actually move in a PG."

FAQ: choosing PG management software

Is generic property management software good enough for a PG?

It can work for basic rent tracking, but most generic tools don't handle per-bed sharing, proration, or resident-level KYC well — you'll end up maintaining a parallel spreadsheet for the parts it can't do.

What should PG software cost?

Look for pricing tied to occupied beds rather than a flat fee — it should scale down automatically when beds are empty. See our pricing breakdown for what per-bed pricing looks like in practice.

Do I need a resident app, or is an owner dashboard enough?

An owner dashboard alone still routes every rent question, complaint and mess query through you personally. A resident app is what actually reduces your day-to-day load, not just organises your records.
