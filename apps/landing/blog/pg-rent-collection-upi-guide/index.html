How to Collect PG Rent Online: A UPI Guide for Hostel & PG Owners

Rent collection is the one job in a PG that touches every resident, every month. Get it wrong and you're chasing payments on WhatsApp until the 10th. Here's how to set up a flow residents actually follow.

B Basera Team 4 Aug 2026 5 min read

Most PGs in India already collect rent over UPI — a resident sends money to the owner's personal or business UPI ID, maybe sends a screenshot on WhatsApp as proof. It works, until you're running more than a handful of beds. Then it quietly turns into an unpaid second job: chasing screenshots, cross-checking your bank statement against a notebook, and never being quite sure who's actually paid this month.

None of that means you need a payment gateway. It means you need a system around the UPI payment you're already collecting. Here's what that system looks like.

1. Fix a due date and stick to it

Pick one due date for the whole property — the 1st or the 5th works well for most PGs — rather than each resident's individual move-in anniversary. A single due date means one reminder cycle, one collection push, and one day you can look at your books and know exactly who's outstanding. Anniversary-based billing feels "fair" but it means you're never not chasing someone.

2. Generate the invoice before you ask for the money

Don't ask "please pay rent" — send a specific number: this month's rent, any pending dues, minus anything you owe them (a referral credit, a partial refund). A resident is far more likely to pay quickly against a clear, itemised amount than a vague reminder. This is also the single biggest reason spreadsheet-run PGs fall behind: prorating a mid-month move-in or a room change by hand is exactly the kind of arithmetic that gets deferred to "later."

3. Put your UPI ID (and a QR) in one place residents can always find

Not buried in an old WhatsApp message from three months ago. Every resident should be able to open one place — an app, a pinned message, a laminated notice near the mess — and get your current UPI ID or QR code without asking you for it. Every time a resident has to ask "bhaiya what's the UPI ID again," that's a delay measured in days, not minutes.

4. Make "proof of payment" a two-tap action, not a hunt

Residents already take a screenshot after paying — the friction is where it goes next. If it's buried in a WhatsApp group with forty other messages, you'll spend your evening scrolling to match names to screenshots. Give residents one place to upload the screenshot against their own invoice, and give yourself one queue to approve or reject it. This is the exact gap Basera's resident app closes — pay, screenshot, upload, done, with the manager approving in one tap from a dashboard instead of a chat thread.

The money never has to route through a third party for the collection process to be organised. UPI stays UPI — you're just replacing the WhatsApp thread around it with a system.

5. Automate the reminder, not the relationship

A reminder two days before the due date, and a second one the day after, catches most residents without you having to personally nag anyone. Keep the tone neutral and automatic — it's far less awkward for both sides than a manual "hey, rent?" text from the owner. Save your personal follow-up for the residents who are genuinely overdue past a week; that's where a conversation actually helps.

6. Know your real collection number, not your guessed one

"Most people have paid" isn't a number you can run a PG on. At month-end you want one screen that shows: invoiced, collected, and pending, per resident. That single view is what turns rent collection from a monthly scramble into a five-minute check-in. It's also what tells you, early, if a resident is drifting from "forgot" into "can't pay" — a very different conversation, and one you want to start before the deposit is the only thing standing behind three months of rent.

FAQ: PG rent collection

Do I need a payment gateway to collect rent online?

No. Residents can keep paying you directly over UPI — a gateway only adds a transaction cut for a problem (organising the proof and the reminder) that a proper collection flow already solves.

What's the biggest mistake PGs make with rent collection?

Billing each resident on their own move-in anniversary instead of one fixed due date for the whole property. It multiplies your reminder and reconciliation work by however many different dates you're tracking.

How do I handle a resident who moved in mid-month?

Prorate the first month's rent by the number of active days rather than charging a full month or waiving it.
