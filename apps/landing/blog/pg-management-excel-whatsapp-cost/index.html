The Hidden Cost of Running a PG on WhatsApp and Excel

It costs nothing to open a spreadsheet and a WhatsApp group. That's exactly why it's easy to miss what it's actually costing you every month.

B Basera Team 10 Sep 2026 6 min read

Almost every PG starts this way — a resident list in Excel, rent tracked in another sheet, a WhatsApp group for announcements and complaints, and payment screenshots collected wherever they land. It works fine at 10 beds. The problem isn't that this setup is bad software; it's that it isn't software at all, and every gap it leaves gets filled by your own time and memory instead.

The reminder that never gets sent

In a spreadsheet, a rent reminder is something you have to remember to send, to the right person, on the right day. Multiply that by 30, 60, 100 residents and it stops being reliable — not because you're disorganised, but because a human remembering to do the same task for a hundred different people, every month, on time, isn't a realistic expectation. The residents who slip past your memory aren't the ones who refuse to pay — they're the ones nobody reminded.

The screenshot that gets buried

A payment screenshot dropped into a WhatsApp group with residents, staff and everyone else in it is effectively unsearchable by the time you need it — three weeks later, reconciling who's paid. You end up scrolling, or asking the resident to resend it, or just trusting your spreadsheet's "paid" checkbox and hoping you ticked it correctly at the time. See our UPI rent collection guide for what replaces this specific gap.

The vacancy that sits a week longer than it should

If tracking upcoming move-outs means scrolling back through old messages to remember who gave notice and when, you'll often only realize a bed is vacant the week it happens — not the week before, when you could have already had someone lined up. That's a week of lost rent on that bed, and it repeats every time a resident leaves without an active pipeline behind them. More in how to reduce vacant beds.

The deposit dispute with no paper trail

Six months after a resident moves in, "what did we agree the deposit deduction policy was" is not a question a spreadsheet answers — it's a question you either remember correctly or don't. A move-out settlement with no documented move-in condition, no itemised deduction, is the single most common source of a bad review from an otherwise fine tenancy. See our security deposit guide for the paperwork that prevents this.

The spreadsheet doesn't cost you money directly. It costs you the reminder that didn't go out, the bed that sat empty an extra week, and the dispute that didn't need to happen.

The compounding cost of "it mostly works"

None of these gaps are catastrophic individually — a late reminder here, a slow-to-fill bed there. What makes the spreadsheet-and-WhatsApp setup expensive isn't any single failure, it's that these small gaps repeat every month, at every property, for as long as the setup stays manual. A PG that's "mostly on top of things" with this system is often losing more, quietly, than the cost of switching to something purpose-built.

Where the line actually is

There's no fixed bed count where a spreadsheet "stops working" — it's more about how many things you're tracking simultaneously. Once you're juggling rent status, KYC documents, complaints and upcoming vacancies across more than a handful of residents, the coordination overhead itself becomes the job, ahead of actually running the property.

A quick honest check Could you tell someone, right now, exactly how many beds are vacant, who's overdue on rent, and whose deposit documentation is incomplete — without opening three different places to check? If not, that's the gap costing you, not any single missed payment.

FAQ: PG spreadsheets vs. software

At what size does a PG actually need software?

There's no fixed threshold, but most owners feel the strain once they're tracking more than roughly 20-30 beds manually, or the moment they add a second property — see managing multiple PG properties.

Isn't a free spreadsheet cheaper than paid software?

The spreadsheet itself is free — the missed reminders, extended vacancies and deposit disputes it doesn't prevent aren't. Priced per occupied bed, purpose-built software often costs less than what one avoidable extra vacancy day costs you.

Can I switch from Excel without a painful migration?

Most of what you need to bring over — residents, beds, current rent status — is a one-time import, not a rebuild. It's usually far less disruptive than owners expect.
