KYC for PG & Hostel Residents in India: What Owners Should Collect

Skipping KYC feels like it saves a resident (and you) a step at move-in. It's also how owners end up unable to answer a basic question — who is actually living here — when it matters most.

B Basera Team 25 Aug 2026 6 min read

This isn't legal advice — verification requirements for tenants and paying guests vary by state and, often, by local police jurisdiction, so confirm the current requirement with your local police station or municipal office. What follows is the practical baseline most well-run PGs collect regardless of exact local rules, and why it's worth doing properly even where it isn't strictly mandated.

What to collect from every resident

  • Government photo ID — Aadhaar, PAN, passport, voter ID or driving licence. Aadhaar is the most common, but don't insist on exactly one document type; accept any valid photo ID.
  • A recent photograph — separate from the ID scan, taken at move-in. Useful for the bed map, the resident app profile, and simple visual confirmation at the gate.
  • Permanent address proof — helps in the rare case you need to contact family, and is often part of local tenant-verification paperwork.
  • Emergency contact — a phone number for a parent, guardian or close contact, not just the resident's own number.
  • Employer or institution details — company ID / offer letter for working residents, college ID for students. This is less about verification and more about knowing who to reasonably reach if something goes wrong.

Why tenant verification matters beyond "the rules"

Many Indian states and local police departments require landlords and PG owners to submit tenant details for verification — the exact process (an online portal, a physical form at the local station) differs by city, so check what applies where your PG operates. Beyond compliance, there's a simpler reason to do this properly: it's the single biggest deterrent against a PG being used as a temporary, untraceable address by someone you'd otherwise have no way to identify later.

KYC isn't paperwork for its own sake — it's the difference between "I don't know who that was" and having an actual answer if you're ever asked.

Where KYC most often goes wrong

Collecting it, then losing track of it

A photo of an Aadhaar card sitting in a phone's camera roll or a WhatsApp chat isn't really "on file" — it's one lost phone away from being gone, and one shared-computer login away from being exposed to someone it shouldn't be. If you can't find a specific resident's ID in under a minute, it isn't actually organised.

Treating verification as a one-time box to tick

A document collected at move-in and never looked at again misses the actual value of KYC, which is having it available exactly when you need to reference it — a dispute, a police enquiry, a lost-item claim. It should be tied to the specific resident and bed, retrievable the same day you need it.

Storing sensitive documents with no access control

ID documents, addresses and emergency contacts are exactly the data a PG can't afford to handle casually. A shared drive folder anyone with the link can open, or a WhatsApp group with forty other people in it, isn't secure storage — it's a liability waiting to surface.

A simple move-in checklist Photo ID (front + back if applicable) → recent photograph → permanent address → emergency contact → employer/institution proof. Collect all five before handing over keys, not "whenever they get around to it" after move-in — the latter is how KYC quietly never gets completed.

What good KYC storage looks like

Documents should be attached to the specific resident's profile (not a general folder), accessible to the manager who needs them and no one else, and ideally encrypted rather than sitting as plain image files. If you're evaluating PG management software, this is one of the features worth actually testing — upload a document, then check who else in the system can see it.

FAQ: PG resident KYC

Is KYC legally mandatory for PG residents?

Tenant/paying-guest verification requirements vary by state and city — check with your local police station or municipal office for what applies to you. Regardless of the legal requirement, collecting basic ID and emergency contact information is standard good practice for any PG.

What if a resident doesn't have Aadhaar?

Accept any valid government photo ID — PAN, passport, voter ID or driving licence all work. Don't make Aadhaar the only accepted document; it can unnecessarily block a legitimate resident.

How long should I keep a resident's KYC documents after they move out?

There's no universal rule — a reasonable practice is to retain records for a period after move-out in case of a later dispute or enquiry, then remove them. Check any state-specific retention guidance that applies to your PG.
